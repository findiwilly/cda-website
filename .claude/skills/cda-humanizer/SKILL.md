# SKILL: CDA Humanizer — Write Like a Person, Not a Program

Use this skill whenever writing or editing ANY user-facing text on CDA projects: website copy, headlines, CTAs, emails, WhatsApp messages, social captions, proposals, blog posts — in French or English.

## The goal
A reader should feel a specific Cameroonian founder wrote this — someone confident, warm, direct, who knows Yaoundé's streets and its businesses. Never a template. Never a machine.

## Banned: the AI accent (delete on sight, FR and EN)
- Openers: "In today's digital landscape…", "Dans le monde numérique d'aujourd'hui…", "In an ever-evolving world…", "Que vous soyez… ou…"
- Filler transitions: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "De plus", "En outre", "Par ailleurs" (as paragraph glue)
- Empty intensifiers: "seamless", "cutting-edge", "révolutionnaire", "innovant", "state-of-the-art", "solutions sur mesure", "world-class" (show it, never claim it)
- The triad tic: three parallel adjectives/phrases in a row ("fast, reliable, and affordable"). One strong word beats three weak ones.
- Symmetric paragraph rhythm: every paragraph the same length with a tidy summary sentence. Humans are uneven.
- Em-dash overuse, "it's not just X, it's Y" constructions, rhetorical questions as section openers, exclamation marks in body copy.
- Generic empathy: "We understand that running a business can be challenging."

## Required: the human accent
1. **Specificity over abstraction.** Not "we help businesses grow" → "votre menu, commandable sur WhatsApp en 30 secondes." Name streets, quartiers, prices in XAF, real situations (délestage, taxi WiFi, MoMo).
2. **Rhythm variety.** Mix short punches with longer sentences. One-line paragraphs are allowed. Fragments too. Like this.
3. **Address one person.** "Vous" as one owner reading on their phone at 22h — not "businesses" or "clients" in the abstract.
4. **Verbs over adjectives.** "Ce site charge en 1,2 s" beats "un site ultra-rapide."
5. **Claims carry numbers or proof.** If neither exists, cut the claim.
6. **Confidence without hedging.** No "we try to", "we aim to", "nous nous efforçons de". Say what happens: "Votre site est en ligne en 14 jours."
7. **Cameroonian French, not Parisian corporate.** Natural register, warm but professional. Read every FR sentence aloud mentally — if a Yaoundé business owner wouldn't say it, rewrite it.
8. **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence needs a second comma, it usually wants to be two sentences.

## The tests before shipping any copy
- **The aloud test:** would the founder say this sentence to a client's face? If it would sound strange spoken, rewrite.
- **The swap test:** could this paragraph sit on a competitor's site unchanged? If yes, it says nothing — rewrite with specifics only CDA would know.
- **The screenshot test:** if someone screenshotted this paragraph alone, would it still sound like a person? 
- **The trim test:** delete the first sentence of the draft. 8 times out of 10 it was throat-clearing.

## Voice anchors (from CDA brand foundation)
Confident, not arrogant — "Votre site est en ligne le 24." never "Nous ferons de notre mieux."
Warm, not casual — "On a vu votre message — voici le plan." never "lol oui on gère."
Clear, not clever — state prices and timelines plainly; curiosity gaps are for strategy details, never for costs.
